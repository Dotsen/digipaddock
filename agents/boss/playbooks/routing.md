# Routing

1. `kd-boss-board revision --card KD-<n>`, then `kd-task view KD-<n>`; the card's meaning is in its text — `kd-task scope KD-<n>`, page by page to its `end (…)` line.
2. No assignee or another project's assignee: `assign --assignee` the route `kd-task projects` names for the card's project, give the project's route as the reason in the owner's language. A part of an agent's task that only the owner can do — a secret or a login of his own account, a DB write, his decision — goes to `@owner`, whatever the project's route. A missing authority (`capability_missing`: a right, a host, a command or a diagnostic an agent's policy lacks) is not such a part: it is platform work, a change of a repository. `set-project` the card to the project that holds the missing thing (the platform's code and registries, or the deployment's values) and `assign` by that project's route, give the `capability_missing` reason and the receiving project (`set-project` works in Inbox only: for a card already in work, `create-inbox` the missing thing as a card of that project instead); the owner's part of it is his approval of the card and his signature on the change, never the card itself. A card at `@owner` wakes no agent: nobody works on it until he does. The owner's new route of a project counts for new cards only: an open card keeps its assignee (watch raises no `route_mismatch` for it). A proposal or a grant made over the old route is refused with `route_mismatch` — read `kd-task projects` and propose again. When the card's kind of work is not the agent's default specialization (a review, tests from the acceptance criteria, documentation), name it in the same write: `assign --assignee @<agent> --spec <name>`; `kd-boss-board specs` lists what each agent holds. A `spec_mismatch` event means the label does not fit its assignee — fix it the same way. Kinds of work and their labels:
   | The card asks for | Assignee and label |
   |---|---|
   | Independent review of a specific brief version (a separate approved review card, its own fresh session; author target and observed repo commit as inputs) | `@analyst`, `spec:brief-review` |
   | Preflight review of an exact diff and operations/checks/rollback plan (no approval or installation) | `@analyst`, `spec:release-check` |
   | Verification after an authorized installation: observed version, services, logs and the affected scenario | `@sysadmin`, `spec:post-deploy`; persistent session with existing read channels |
   | A read-only investigation of a monitor incident P-n: evidence, hypotheses, impact, the next step (no server change) | `@sysadmin`, `spec:incident-triage`; the cabinet labels its own `monitor:investigation` cards itself |
   | A read-only audit of a server against its documentation (the deployment's servers.md, project documents): drifts, what to fix, what stayed unchecked | `@sysadmin`, `spec:drift-audit` |
   | A read-only capacity check of a server: disk, memory, load, database size, a forecast and one recommendation | `@sysadmin`, `spec:capacity` |
   | Bring a new VPN server into service by the deployment's new VPN server checklist (a plan the owner signs; status and evidence per step) | `@sysadmin`, `spec:vpn-deploy` |
   | A review of a commit, a branch or a range in a project's repository at the owner's request (the card names the project and the revision; findings with file, line and severity) | `@analyst`, `spec:commit-review` |
   | A review of a product's daily data-health summary: its warning and failure slices, what dropped and why (a report on the card; no repair) | `@analyst`, `spec:data-health`; the weekly card comes from the Monday digest (`digest.md`) |
   | Draft answer to a customer/partner, grounded in provided current documents and permitted facts; owner sends | `@analyst`, `spec:support-draft` |
   | Onboarding material for a customer or partner who connects — an instruction, a checklist, a letter, from the project documentation (drafts; the owner sends) | `@analyst`, `spec:onboarding` |
   | A text for a channel — a post, a newsletter, an announcement, a sales text (a draft on the card; the owner publishes) | `@analyst`, `spec:copywriting` |
   | A text that lives in a repository — site pages, interface strings, docs copy (a pull request) | `@dev`, `spec:site-copy` |
   | A UX review of an interface — scenarios, widths, the owner's screenshots | `@analyst`, `spec:ux-review` |
   | A report on the agents' spending over a period — tokens, cache share, estimated cost per agent and model, savings to propose (on the owner's card) | `@analyst`, `spec:cost-report` |
   | A design of screens — states, widths, tokens, a prototype in the repository (no product code) | `@dev`, `spec:ui-design` |
   | UI code — a change of the cabinet's or a site's frontend | `@dev`, `spec:implementer`; a worker job on it — `--skill frontend` |

   A label from this table goes only to an agent `kd-boss-board specs` lists it for; a kind of work it does not list yet (marketing) gets no guessed label: assign it without `--spec` (the type's default) and name the gap in the report.
3. The project itself is wrong by the card's meaning (server work at the developer, code at the sysadmin): `set-project` first, then `assign` — in Inbox only; a card the owner already started keeps its project, tell him; tell the owner in one line which project and assignee received KD-<n> and why.
4. Several To Do cards at one agent: set `priority` by due date, then dependencies, then the owner's word. An agent takes one card at a time.
5. A card that cannot be started as written — no acceptance criteria, or the label `needs-analysis` — goes to the analyst at once, without asking anyone: `request-brief --card KD-<n> --revision <rev> --reason "<what the brief must settle>"` (the card stays in Inbox with its project — preparing a brief is not starting the work). A card the analyst already holds gets your addition the same way: it reaches the analyst as a clarification, nothing restarts. The analyst talks with the owner, writes the brief into the card and hands it to the implementer itself, with the next step's `spec:` label, in one write; the card stays in Inbox. A `brief_ready` event (label `brief-ready`) says the brief is done: `propose-promote --reason "<brief ready and one line from the description, in the owner’s language>"`, tell the owner its number `KD-<n>` (no address), and leave the card alone — an edit after the proposal (`set-labels` included) voids it; the label stays. A brief of size S (label `size:S`, KD-9249) starts by itself: the cabinet approves its version and moves the card to To Do on its implementer — a card no longer in Inbox needs no proposal; an S brief the cabinet kept in Inbox (its thread says why: operations, stages, server work, open dependencies) is proposed as any other. A large card with a clear brief: propose a split to the owner as a list of titles. After the owner's «да» — `prepare-create` and `create-inbox` for each (`creating.md`), `set-dependency` where order matters. New cards land in Inbox; to start one, `propose-promote` it and tell the owner its number — his approval in the cabinet moves it.
6. A large goal whose parts the owner should not approve one by one: `propose-delegate` it and tell the owner its number (the owner sees the limits on the decision in the cabinet). After «Разрешить» (the `[grant] KD-<n>: …` wake-up, with the grant's term, quotas and projects): break the goal down yourself, `create-child` each part on its project (it starts at once), `set-dependency` the goal on its children, follow them with `kd-nudge activity` and directives. A refusal (quota, project, revoked grant) goes to the owner, never around the grant. All children Done — tell the owner the goal can be closed.
7. Duplicates: close an unstarted Inbox duplicate yourself, keeping its wishes on the canonical card (`disputes.md`). Never use a dependency for a duplicate.
8. A new card — only after the look of `creating.md`.
