# The factory's operations: `kd-factory`

Read this when a release leaves generated agents outdated, when the cage of your PR names an agent or a platform component to install, or when a card needs `kd-factory` at all.

`kd-work finish KD-<n> --outcome wait_dependency --operation <id>` waits for a factory operation (a reinstall the release or `kd-factory` asked for; no dependency card needed): its end wakes you — a failed one as «ended without the answer you needed».

- `kd-factory` — the factory's operations on your own card of a project the factory serves (`digipaddock`, `digipaddock-deploy`), its catalog only (`common/coordination/factory.json`): `agent.reinstall` of generated agents on this host (drain the role's sessions and jobs with its queue kept, `new_agent.sh --install`, check, resume) and `component.install` of a platform component in one of its fixed modes on {{host.stage_alias}} (`repo-attach` install|check, `transcribe` install|check, `e2e-runtime` install|check, `devcheck` install|check, `session-host` check|restart — the restart waits until every role's turns end). Nothing else — other units and installers, `deploy_target.sh`, apt, reboots, servers, prod and the nodes are not in it (servers stay the sysadmin's; the rest is the owner's word). `kd-factory plan KD-<n> --operations '<JSON list>' --intent "…" --basis "…"` asks the owner to approve the steps (`[{"verb": "agent.reinstall", "target": "factory", "agents": ["dev"], "what": "…", "effect": "…", "risk": {"level": "low", "why": "…"}, "rollback": "…"}]`; then `kd-work finish KD-<n> --outcome wait_user`); an approved brief whose «Операции» lists them approves them too. `kd-factory reinstall KD-<n> --agents a[,b] --key <key>` requests what the approval covers (the same key answers the same operation) and prints its id — end the turn with `kd-work finish KD-<n> --outcome wait_dependency --operation <id> --note "…"`; never wait in the turn. A component step is `{"verb": "component.install", "target": "factory", "component": "repo-attach", "mode": "install", "what": "…", "effect": "…", "risk": {…}, "rollback": "…"}`: the grant covers the tree deployed when the owner signs — ask after the release that brings the code; a release after the signature needs a new plan. A step that must run right after the release of this card's own PR is written with `"revision": "release"` instead (in your plan on the card of the PR — on a stage card, the stage's own plan; a brief with stages carries no such step): one signature covers it, the release itself requests it on the revision it brings, and `kd-factory install` on such a step refuses (`is approved for the release of this card — the release requests it`). `kd-factory install KD-<n> --component <c> --mode <m> --key <key>` runs it (again after a fix on the same revision, no new signature) and prints the id — end the turn the same way; the end posts the exit codes and the masked output tail in the card's thread. Its result is the runner's fact about the script and its check, not the card's acceptance: the protocol's own checks after it (`kd-host-read`, a probe through the cabinet) stay yours. `kd-factory status <id>` — its state and per-agent or component result. Each role installs as soon as its own turns and jobs end, the others do not wait for it — your own role after this turn and your other slot's; a role still at work past the drain's limit is skipped (`drain_timeout`): do not ask again in the same breath — the card's next turn decides whether it is still needed. A failed install (or host check) keeps the role paused and tells the owner.
